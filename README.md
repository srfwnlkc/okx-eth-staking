## What Ethereum staking actually does

Ethereum uses a proof-of-stake consensus system. Instead of miners competing with computing power, validators deposit ETH and perform duties such as proposing blocks and confirming transactions.

A validator that follows the rules can earn rewards. A validator that is offline, behaves incorrectly, or signs conflicting information can lose part of its stake through penalties or slashing. The network uses staked ETH as an economic incentive: validators have something valuable at risk, so dishonest behavior becomes expensive.

Running a validator directly generally requires **32 ETH**, dedicated hardware or a reliable server, stable internet access, validator software, secure key management, and ongoing maintenance. The technical burden is manageable for experienced users, but it is not a “set it and forget it” savings account.

Ethereum’s official staking documentation distinguishes between solo staking, delegated services, staking pools, and liquid staking. Each method lowers a different barrier, but each introduces its own risks and responsibilities.

For smaller holders, pooled or exchange staking removes the 32 ETH requirement. You contribute a smaller amount, while the provider combines user deposits and manages validator infrastructure.

## What BETH means for liquidity

The main reason users choose liquid staking products is to avoid holding an asset that is completely tied up in a validator process.

When you stake through OKX, your ETH is represented by BETH. This does not mean that BETH is risk-free or identical to holding ETH in a private wallet. It means that the token represents your staked position within the OKX product.

According to OKX, BETH can be used in several ways:

- Trade BETH against ETH or USDT.
- Use BETH as collateral for supported loans and trading products.
- Enter certain campaigns where BETH is accepted.
- Continue receiving staking-related rewards while holding eligible BETH.

The practical advantage is flexibility. You may be able to trade BETH instead of waiting for the underlying staking redemption process. The practical risk is that any market-traded representation of staked ETH can have its own liquidity conditions. A secondary-market price is not automatically guaranteed to match the value of the underlying asset at every moment.

Ethereum.org makes a similar distinction for liquid staking tokens generally: a tokenized staking position can be traded on the market, but its price can deviate from the underlying ETH value during periods of stress or thin liquidity.

In other words, “liquid” does not mean “instant, guaranteed withdrawal at the exact ETH price.” It means there is a token or market mechanism that may provide an alternative route to liquidity.

## Ethereum staking compared with other methods

OKX is convenient, but it is not automatically the right option for every ETH holder.

### Solo staking

Solo staking provides the most direct relationship with Ethereum. You control the validator infrastructure and keys, and you do not hand custody to an exchange or pool.

The trade-off is operational responsibility. You need at least 32 ETH for a standard validator, reliable uptime, secure backups, client maintenance, monitoring, and a clear understanding of validator withdrawal credentials. A configuration mistake can reduce rewards or create penalties.

Solo staking may suit an experienced Ethereum user who values control more than convenience. It is excessive for someone who simply wants to put 0.2 ETH to work without managing a server.

### Staking pools

Pools combine deposits from multiple users so that smaller holders can participate. Some pools issue liquid staking tokens, while others use an off-chain accounting system.

Ethereum.org notes that pooled staking introduces an intermediary between the user and the Ethereum protocol. Smart-contract pools add contract and governance risks; centralized products add custody and provider risks.

Pools can offer more self-custody than an exchange if you hold the liquid staking token in your own wallet. They can also require more wallet and DeFi knowledge.

### Liquid staking protocols

Liquid staking protocols issue a token representing your position. You retain the ability to transfer or trade that token, but the token may trade above or below the value of the underlying ETH.

Risks include smart-contract exploits, governance changes, validator penalties, operator concentration, and market depegging. Ethereum.org specifically warns that liquid staking adds layers of risk beyond the base Ethereum staking risk.

### Exchange staking

Exchange staking has the lowest technical barrier. You do not need to run a node, monitor validator software, or store validator keys yourself.

The price is custody. Your position depends on the exchange’s account controls, product operations, redemption procedures, and regional access. This may be a reasonable trade for beginners, active traders, and users who already keep a portion of their crypto on an exchange. It is less suitable for users whose first priority is self-custody and independent verification.

Choose OKX ETH staking when:

- You have less than 32 ETH.
- You do not want to run validator hardware.
- You want daily rewards.
- You prefer an exchange interface.
- You may want to trade or use BETH as collateral.

Consider solo staking when:

- You have at least 32 ETH.
- You can manage validator infrastructure.
- You want control of validator keys and operations.
- You are comfortable handling technical maintenance and withdrawal settings.

Consider a self-custodied staking pool when:

- You want liquid staking exposure.
- You understand smart-contract and depeg risk.
- You are comfortable managing a wallet and interacting with decentralized applications.
- You prefer not to keep the staking position entirely on a centralized exchange.

Keep ETH unstaked when:

- You need immediate access to the funds.
- You are still deciding whether you want long-term ETH exposure.
- A price move would make you panic-sell.
- You do not understand the redemption terms.
- The expected reward is not worth the custody and liquidity trade-off for your situation.
